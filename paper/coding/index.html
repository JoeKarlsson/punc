Semicolon and colon coding

The rule

Each passage has one semicolon or colon highlighted. Ask: could a writer replace that mark with a period and start a new sentence? That means the words on each side are clauses that could each stand alone as a sentence.

  • Joins two sentences: yes. Count the part after the mark as standalone even if it opens with and, but, for, nor, yet, so, however or therefore.
  • Does not: what follows is an item in a list or series (including a run of "that ..." clauses), a relative or subordinate clause or phrase (which, whose, because, when, if), a fragment or participle phrase, a quotation or list the mark introduces, or a heading, footnote, citation, ratio or other non-prose.
  • If the passage is too short to tell, make your best call and tick Unsure.

Passages are cut about 110 characters either side of the mark, sometimes mid-word, and keep the source's spelling. Your answers save in this browser as you go; when you finish, press Export CSV and send the file back.
