Artist statement

A lot of what steers you through a book isn't the words. A comma tells you to take a breath, a period tells you to stop, and you follow every one without noticing.

Punc takes the words away and leaves those instructions, so you can see a book's rhythm on its own. Alice's Adventures in Wonderland comes out loud and nervous, full of exclamation points and questions, which is how it feels to read.

It's an erasure, like the blackout poems I make on paper, except Punc keeps none of the words.

How it works

Punc drops every letter and number and keeps the marks: periods, commas, semicolons, dashes, quotes, brackets and about a hundred other symbols. The books are the two thousand English books people downloaded most last month from Project Gutenberg, and the posters are Gutenberg's own covers, dithered to pink and black.

Everything happens in your browser: a file you open on the read page is never uploaded. Visits are counted with a self-hosted copy of Plausible, with no cookies. The code is on GitHub, and the research behind the numbers is in the repo's paper folder.
